\section{Conclusion}

An LLM refinement loop for differential testing depends on domain knowledge
it does not have. We compared four sources of that knowledge under control,
changing nothing but a 1{,}500-character slot in the prompt. A human
characterization was best, a summary written from reading the code was a
reliable second, and a summary written from 20 black-box probes was
all-or-nothing on Dart, where divergence is rare, and clearly helpful on
Kotlin, where it is common. Across arms, the divergence patterns found
largely followed the facts the knowledge stated, and no arm found a pattern
outside the known ones.
A pre-registered extension showed why and how far that can be fixed: an
agent required to cover generic test categories probes where the unguided
agent did not, learns what those probes reveal, and nearly doubles the
unguided probing rate on Dart; a larger model helps less. Both still miss
what their budget never reached. Self-characterization is therefore not yet a
substitute for knowledge where knowledge matters most; it becomes one only
as far as its probes are made to reach the narrow behaviors that matter.
