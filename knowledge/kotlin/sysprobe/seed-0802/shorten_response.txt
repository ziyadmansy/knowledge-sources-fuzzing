1. Missing required fields: Gson and Jackson accept missing fields, defaulting to null or zero; Moshi and kotlinx.serialization reject with MissingFieldException or JsonDataException.  
2. Non-nullable fields set to null: Gson accepts and sets to null or zero; Jackson rejects with KotlinInvalidNullException; Moshi and kotlinx.serialization reject with JsonDataException or JsonDecodingException.  
3. Wrong JSON type for fields: Gson and Moshi accept string fields given as numbers (e.g., "amount": 1), coercing to string; kotlinx.serialization rejects; Jackson accepts with coercion except for nulls.  
4. Numeric field "id" null: Gson and Jackson accept, decode as zero; Moshi and kotlinx.serialization reject.  
5. Enum "status" unknown or case variant: Gson accepts, decodes as null; Moshi, kotlinx.serialization, and Jackson reject with JsonDataException or InvalidFormatException.  
6. Extra unknown keys: Gson and Moshi accept and ignore; kotlinx.serialization rejects with JsonDecodingException; Jackson rejects with UnrecognizedPropertyException.  
7. Duplicate keys: Gson, Moshi, kotlinx.serialization, and Jackson accept, last key wins in decoding.  
8. Nested object fields behave like top-level: Gson and Jackson accept null or missing fields in nested objects; Moshi and kotlinx.serialization reject.  
9. Null arrays ("tags"): Gson accepts null; Jackson rejects; Moshi and kotlinx.serialization reject.  
10. All four accept fully valid documents.
